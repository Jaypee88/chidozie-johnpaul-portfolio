On working with AI tools

Using AI well is its own skill.

I use Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini regularly — for coding, debugging, research, brainstorming, project planning, documentation and building teaching materials. That's not the same as letting them do the work for me. The quality of what comes back depends heavily on how clearly I structure the problem and the instructions in the first place, and that's the part I've had to actually learn: prompt engineering as a practical skill, not a buzzword.

In practice, that means I still own the thinking — the architecture of a model, the judgment calls in a dataset, the structure of a lesson — and I use AI tools to move faster through the parts that benefit from a second pair of hands.

About

Building a career around a specific goal

I'm a Computer Engineering student at Nnamdi Azikiwe University, currently in 400 level, expecting to graduate in 2028. But the more honest starting point is this: I didn't get into AI and machine learning because they're trending. I got into them because I wanted to build things that solve real problems, and they turned out to be the right tools for that.

That distinction matters to me. There are faster ways to monetize a skill in tech than machine learning and data science. I chose this path anyway, because I care more about where it leads than how quickly it pays off.

My long-term interest is healthcare technology — specifically, what AI, machine learning, data and embedded systems can do for healthcare in Nigeria and across Africa. I've always had an interest in medicine, but I discovered early that my real strengths are in mathematics, problem-solving and engineering. Rather than treat that as a detour from healthcare, I decided to approach healthcare through technology instead.

Part of what pushes me here is watching how technological and infrastructural limits can hold back good, qualified people. The problem isn't always a shortage of skill — sometimes it's a shortage of the tools and systems that let that skill do its job. I want to help close that gap, in healthcare and beyond it: in people's livelihoods, finance, education and everyday decision-making.

An engineer shouldn't build things just because they can be built. An engineer should build things that solve meaningful problems.

Learning in public, and teaching what I learn

I spend a lot of time learning, researching, experimenting and building — but I don't think knowledge is worth much if it stays with one person. I've benefited a lot from people who took the time to teach me, so I try to pass that forward. It's part of why teaching and student development keep showing up in my story alongside the technical work.

I also try to stay around people who challenge me to improve. I'd rather be in a room where everyone is pushing each other forward than be the most advanced person in it — seeing people around me do well is motivating, not threatening. Alongside that, I hold onto discipline as a value that isn't limited to academics: the same discipline that gets a research task done also shows up in how I approach programming, engineering and personal growth. My faith is part of that too — not something I lead with, but something that sits underneath the way I try to combine hard work with grace.

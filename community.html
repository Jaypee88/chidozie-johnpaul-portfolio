Community & Teaching

Knowledge only counts if it moves

I've benefited from people who took the time to teach me. Passing that forward is a deliberate part of how I engage with engineering, not an afterthought.

How I teach AI/ML

When I facilitate, I try to get past surface-level definitions. For the Skill Up Program's AI/ML track, I built my own structured Jupyter Notebook material — Markdown explanations, visual walkthroughs, worked examples, and executable practice sections students could run and break themselves. I also lean on tools like Claude to help organize and develop material faster, but I stay responsible for what's actually taught and how it's delivered.

Where I've taught and coordinated

  • Skill Up Program — Co-coordinated the six-week program and personally facilitated the AI/ML track for around 50 students, out of roughly 150–200 students reached overall, in collaboration with ACES.
  • Technical enlightenment program — Coordinated a session for Second-year students, personally taught Proteus, and worked with another facilitator who covered Arduino.

Why I bother

Seeing people around me improve pushes me to improve too. I'd rather be part of a community that grows together than be the only one moving forward.

What the material looks like

Structured Jupyter Notebooks: Markdown explanation → worked example → executable exercise. Built to be read and run, not just read.
